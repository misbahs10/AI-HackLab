Our analysis of nearly 39 000 orders shows that, on average, a delivery takes about 27 minutes and covers a distance of roughly 10 km, giving a typical speed of 24 km/h. The worst traffic condition—heavy congestion—slows deliveries by about 5 minutes (average 31 minutes). When we look at how distance affects time, the moderate positive correlation (0.32) confirms that longer routes do take longer, but other factors also play a role. The biggest problem occurs when bad weather meets traffic: deliveries hit a peak of 37 minutes when fog and jam conditions coincide. In short, reducing traffic congestion and improving route planning during adverse weather will cut delivery times and improve customer satisfaction.
